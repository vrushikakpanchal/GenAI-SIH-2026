QUARTERLY INFRASTRUCTURE SECURITY REPORT

Introduction

This report summarizes findings from the security review of the organization's
public-facing infrastructure conducted during the last quarter. The review covered
network perimeter systems, web applications, and third-party integrations.

Findings

Several unpatched servers were identified running outdated software versions with
known vulnerabilities. The vulnerability in the authentication module could allow
an attacker to bypass login controls under specific conditions. Additionally, two
third-party vendor integrations were found to transmit data without adequate
encryption, creating exposure to interception during transit.

The review also found that logging coverage across critical systems is inconsistent,
which limits the ability to detect and respond to incidents in a timely manner.
Staff awareness of phishing techniques has improved compared to the previous
quarter based on simulated exercises, though click-through rates remain above
the target threshold.

Impact Assessment

If exploited, the authentication vulnerability could result in unauthorized access
to sensitive customer records, leading to regulatory exposure and reputational
damage. The unencrypted vendor data transmission increases the risk of data
interception, particularly for integrations handling financial information.
Inconsistent logging would significantly delay incident detection, increasing the
potential impact of any successful breach and complicating post-incident forensics.

Recommendations

It is recommended that all identified servers be patched within thirty days,
prioritizing the authentication module vulnerability. Vendor contracts should be
reviewed to require encryption in transit for all data integrations, with
non-compliant vendors given ninety days to remediate. Logging infrastructure
should be upgraded to provide consistent, centralized coverage across all
critical systems within the next two quarters. Continued phishing simulation
exercises are advised, with additional targeted training for staff groups
showing higher click-through rates.

Conclusion

While overall security posture has improved modestly since the last review,
the authentication vulnerability and inconsistent logging represent the most
urgent risks requiring executive attention and resource allocation in the
coming quarter.
